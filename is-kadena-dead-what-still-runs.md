**Short answer:** the company ended. The blockchain did not. On 21 October 2025 Kadena LLC said it could no longer continue operating and would stop maintaining the blockchain at once. Independent miners kept producing blocks anyway. Fourteen months later, all twenty chains are still building, and your KDA is still KDA.

That distinction matters more than it sounds, because almost every confused conversation about Kadena in 2026 comes from collapsing four different things into one word: the company, the chain, the token, and the development team.

## You'll see this if

- Search results put "Kadena shut down" next to a block explorer that is still advancing.
- An exchange told you KDA was delisted and you assumed the chain had stopped.
- Someone in a group chat said your KDA is worthless or that you missed a migration.
- You opened an old wallet, saw a balance, and wondered whether the number means anything any more.

## The four things people call "Kadena"

| Thing | State in November 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Kadena LLC, the company | Wound down. It said it could no longer continue business operations and stopped maintaining the blockchain on 21 October 2025. |
| Chainweb, the network | Running. Twenty braided chains, still producing blocks roughly every 30 seconds per chain, maintained by a community team rather than the company. |
| KDA, the token | Unchanged. Same chain, same ticker, same accounts. No migration, no snapshot, no claim process, no replacement token. |
| The core team | Split. Most left with the company; five former leads took over platform keys under a 3-of-5 multisig in November 2025 and publish as the KDA Community Edition. |

Only the first row is a shutdown. The other three are continuations, which is why a flat answer of "yes, Kadena is dead" is wrong in the same way that a flat "no, everything is fine" is wrong.

## What happened, in order

1. **21 October 2025.** Kadena's own account posted that the organisation was "no longer able to continue business operations". The statement pointed out that the blockchain was not owned or operated by the company and that the KDA token and protocol would continue without it.
2. **Late October to December 2025.** Exchanges read that announcement as a risk signal and exited one after another: Bitget on 31 October, Binance.US on 28 October, OKX on 29 October, MEXC on 2 November, KuCoin on 5 November, Binance on 12 November, and Bybit closing withdrawals by 23 December. Each venue set its own withdrawal deadline. None of them were reacting to a chain failure.
3. **6 November 2025.** The replacement node build, version 3.0, appeared on GitHub.
4. **8 November 2025.** At block 6,335,858 the fork went live. Control passed to five former core developers, each holding one share of a multisig that needs three signatures. No balance moved, and no holder had to act.
5. **15 November 2025.** The company-side lineage stopped producing blocks. This is the single most misread date in the whole story: it describes an abandoned branch, not the network everyone else was using.
6. **24 November 2025 and 9 January 2026.** The original node repository and the Chainweaver wallet repository were archived read-only. Archived is not deleted: the code is public, and the community fork is where releases now come from.
7. **2026.** Three more node releases shipped, the most recent being 3.2.1 on 21 August 2026. Chain IDs 0 through 19 are all active.

## What "dead" would actually look like

A useful test is to stop reading headlines and check five conditions. As of November 2026:

| Condition | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Blocks stop being produced | Not true. All twenty chains are building. |
| No working node or API | Not true. The community API answers on `api.chainweb-community.org`; the old company API does not. |
| No wallet can hold or send KDA | Not true. Several wallets still do, including desktop and mobile options and Ledger through a companion tool. |
| No market price at all | Not true, though liquidity is thin and the price is a fraction of its former level. |
| No code development | Partly true of the original repositories, which are archived. Releases continue in the community fork. |

Four and a half of five. That is not a dead chain; it is a chain with no company behind it, which is a genuinely different and much rarer situation.

## How to verify it yourself in five minutes

Do not take any of it on trust. These checks use only public infrastructure:

1. Open a community block explorer and note the latest block height. Write it down.
2. Wait two minutes, refresh, and confirm the height has moved.
3. Call the community API's `/info` endpoint and read the node version field. A version number means a node is answering.
4. Paste your `k:` address into the explorer, select chain 0, and then chain 1. Your balance may exist on several chains at once; that is normal for Chainweb.
5. Check the community node repository's releases page for the most recent tag and its date.

If all five return live data, the network is running and your keys are still your keys. If one of them fails, try a second explorer or endpoint before drawing a conclusion, because a single provider going offline is not a network event.

## What this means for KDA you already hold

- **If your KDA is in a wallet you control,** nothing changed. Your seed phrase still derives the same keys, and the accounts those keys control still hold the same balances. You do not need to "upgrade" anything.
- **If your KDA is still on a delisted exchange,** the chain is not the problem; the venue is. Most withdrawal windows closed between December 2025 and January 2026, and what happens to balances left behind is decided by each exchange's own policy, not by Kadena.
- **If someone offers to migrate or claim your KDA,** stop. There is no migration and there are no claims. Any page, bot or support account asking you to connect a wallet to "recover" or "swap" KDA is impersonating a process that does not exist.

## A note on tone

You will find two camps. One insists the project is dead and anyone still holding is deluded; the other insists nothing happened and the roadmap is on track. Both are skipping the evidence. The verifiable position is narrower and less comfortable: the company is gone, the chain runs on volunteer-maintained software with a five-person key ceremony, liquidity is thin, and the infrastructure you can actually reach has moved to new domains.

That is the honest sentence. Everything else here is a footnote to it.

## FAQ

**Did KDA holders have to do anything in November 2025?**
No. The fork changed who controls platform keysets, not account balances. No action was required, and none is required now.

**Is there a new token or a swap?**
No. Same chain, same ticker, same accounts. Treat any swap or claim offer as hostile.

**Why do so many sites still say Kadena is fine?**
Because many were written before October 2025 and never updated, and because some are marketing pages for products that benefit from the older story. Dates matter more than adjectives; check when a page was last revised.

**Is the price relevant to whether the chain works?**
No. Price reflects demand for the token, not block production. A chain can be technically alive and economically marginal at the same time, and in November 2026 that is roughly where this one sits.

**Can the chain stop in future?**
Yes, in principle. It depends on miners continuing to run nodes and on the community stewards maintaining releases. That risk is real and worth stating plainly rather than hiding behind the word "decentralised".
