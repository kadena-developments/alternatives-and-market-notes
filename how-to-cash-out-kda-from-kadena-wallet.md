Cashing out sounds like one action and is actually three: getting KDA to a venue that will accept a deposit, finding a buyer there, and moving the proceeds into a bank account. Between October 2025 and January 2026 the first two closed at most large exchanges at once, which is why holders who did nothing wrong now describe the process as impossible.

It is not impossible. It is narrower, it requires checking a venue before you move anything, and the order of the checks matters more than the choice of venue.

## You'll see this if

- Your KDA is on an exchange that no longer lets you withdraw it.
- You are looking for a venue after KuCoin, Binance, OKX or MEXC closed.
- You want to realise a loss and cannot find anywhere that will buy.
- You have seen a price page for KDA and assumed that means it is tradable.

## The three legs, and why they fail separately

Every exit runs through the same three legs. A venue can be open on one and closed on another, which is how people end up selling a balance they cannot withdraw.

| Leg | What it needs | How it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Deposit | A venue that still credits KDA to your account | Deposits disabled before trading is |
| 2. Trade | An order book with a real bid | Pair visible, no volume |
| 3. Off-ramp | A withdrawal channel to a bank or card | Withdrawal suspended after a delisting notice |

The failure that catches people is leg 1 and leg 3 moving in opposite directions. A venue disables deposits first and withdrawals last, so a balance that was already there can still leave while a new deposit cannot arrive.

## The check that saves the transfer

Do this before any deposit, every time, in this order.

1. **Open the venue's KDA deposit page** and confirm the deposit address is generated, not displayed as unavailable.
2. **Note which chain the venue uses.** Exchange deposit addresses are chain-specific, and Kadena has twenty chains. A deposit sent to chain 0 when the venue credits chain 2 is not recoverable by support.
3. **Read the minimum deposit and any memo or tag requirement.** Both are common causes of a credited-but-invisible balance.
4. **Send a small test amount first.** Wait for it to be credited before sending the rest. This single habit prevents most losses in this process.
5. **Check the withdrawal channel before you sell, not after.** Selling into a balance you cannot withdraw converts a price problem into a trapped-funds problem.

If the venue cannot answer steps 1 and 5 from its own pages, it is not a route. It is a maybe.

## Selling when a buyer exists

Where a spot market is live, the mechanics are ordinary: deposit, sell into the quote currency, then withdraw. Two details deserve attention.

**The quote currency is not the exit.** Selling KDA for USDT leaves you holding USDT on the same venue. The off-ramp is the step that turns that into a bank balance, and it is the step most often suspended during a delisting cycle.

**Withdrawal limits apply to the fiat leg, not the crypto leg.** A venue can let you withdraw USDT while fiat withdrawal requires verification you have not completed. Finishing verification before you need it is the difference between a same-day exit and a week of support tickets.

For holders outside the United States, regional venues price in local currency, and the spread on a thin book is often wider than the fee schedule suggests. Compare the effective rate, not the headline fee.

## If no venue will take a deposit

Three options remain, and none of them is comfortable.

**Hold.** The chain still produces blocks, the accounts still exist, and the token is unchanged. Holding does not fix a loss for tax purposes, and it does not create a buyer.

**Transfer to someone who wants it.** A transfer is a disposal, and a disposal is the event that fixes a gain or loss. This requires a counterparty you trust and a price you can document.

**Treat the position as worthless, only if it genuinely is.** This is where most people's assumptions are wrong. The IRS addressed it directly in Chief Counsel Memorandum 202302011, released 13 January 2023: a decline in value is not a deductible loss, and "a decrease in value must be accompanied by some affirmative step that fixes the amount of the loss, such as abandonment, sale, or exchange". An asset that still trades anywhere, at any price above zero, is not worthless on the authority's own reasoning.

That is what the published guidance says, not advice about your situation. Individual facts decide individual cases, and a tax professional is the right person to apply them.

## Why this became hard

The exits did not close because the chain failed. They closed because the company that ran it announced on 21 October 2025 that it was ceasing operations, and venues that had listed KDA for its issuer relationship had no reason to keep a thin market open.

The chain continued under a community multisig, the token did not migrate, and accounts were untouched. What did not continue was the market-making and listing support that a funded company provides. That is the gap a holder is standing in when they ask where to sell.

## Bridges and swaps, stated plainly

If a venue will not take a deposit, the next instinct is a bridge or an on-chain swap. Two facts are worth having before that instinct is acted on.

The community bridge interface at kinesis.kda-chain.org carries its own banner: "Community version is in test. Use with care." A bridge described that way by the people running it is a beta, and a beta handling a balance you cannot replace is a risk you are choosing rather than a service you are using.

The exchange that once anchored on-chain trading here no longer responds at either of its addresses. What replaced it is thinner, and a thin pair means the price you see is not the price a modest order gets. Neither fact makes on-chain routes illegitimate. Both make them a poor last resort for a large position.

## Handling what is left

- **Verify the funds still exist on-chain** before believing a zero balance in an app. Walk the account across every chain the wallet can switch to.
- **Do not pay a recovery service.** No third party can move funds from an account it does not control, and the offers that promise otherwise are the same template as the ones that arrive by direct message.
- **Keep records of acquisition and disposal.** Dates, amounts, venues and prices. If a loss is ever realised, the documentation is the claim.
- **Do not send KDA to a contract or address you cannot identify** in the hope of converting it. Bridge interfaces and "swap" sites that appear after a delisting are a standard phishing pattern.

## FAQ

**Can I still sell KDA in the United States?**
Some venues still show a market, but availability changes quickly and several require accounts that were opened before the delisting wave. Confirm the deposit and withdrawal channels on the venue's own pages before moving anything.

**Is Coinbase going to list KDA?**
Nothing published by Coinbase suggests it, and KDA has never been tradable there. A price page is not a listing announcement.

**My exchange balance vanished after a delisting. What now?**
Check whether withdrawals are still open, and check the withdrawal deadline in the delisting notice. If the deadline passed, the venue's own notice is the only authority on what happens next.

**Does selling on a decentralised exchange avoid these problems?**
It replaces a venue check with a contract check. Liquidity on Kadena pairs is thin, and the interfaces that survive a delisting are not always the ones that were audited. Verify before connecting a wallet.

**Is the loss deductible if I simply stop looking at it?**
No. Under the guidance cited above, a decline in value is not a loss until something fixes it: a sale, an exchange, an abandonment, or genuine worthlessness.
